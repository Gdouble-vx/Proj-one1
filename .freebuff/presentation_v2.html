🎯 Problem Statement

OSPF Limitations

OSPF (Open Shortest Path First) routes traffic based on hop count only — it treats all links equally regardless of bandwidth, utilization, or congestion.

1
Asymmetric bandwidth ignored — 15 Mbps narrow links = 200 Mbps wide links in OSPF's view
2
No congestion awareness — traffic funnels through bottleneck links
3
No dynamic adaptation — static weights don't respond to traffic changes

Training Challenges

Training DRL on real SDN (Mininet + ONOS REST API) takes 29.2 hours for 100K steps (~1 FPS). Models converge to "safe" actions (action=1.0 everywhere) due to heavy penalties.

NSFNET Topology (Standard Benchmark)

National Science Foundation Network — used in RouteNet [IEEE JSAC 2020], DRL+GNN [arXiv 2022], PPO+GNN [JNCA 2025]

Switches14 (3-tier hierarchy)
Links21 bidirectional
Narrow links (<30 Mbps)6 links (15-20 Mbps)
Wide links (≥100 Mbps)12 links (100-200 Mbps)
Bandwidth range15 — 200 Mbps (asymmetric)
⚠️ OSPF shortest-path routes go through narrow bottleneck links (15 Mbps) while wide bypass routes (100-200 Mbps) are underutilized.

Our Goal

Use PPO + GNN (Graph Attention Network) to learn optimal link weights that route traffic through wide links and avoid narrow bottlenecks, improving throughput, reducing latency and packet loss compared to OSPF.

NSFNET: Farrington & Helios 1992; Rusek et al. IEEE JSAC 20202 / 12

Reward Shaping (4 Components)

📈 Metric reward: throughput^1.2 / latency
🔍 Exploration bonus: reward weight changes from previous step
🎲 Diversity bonus: reward non-uniform actions (penalize safe play)
🆕 Novelty bonus: reward deviation from OSPF baseline (all=1.0)

💡 Key Findings

PPO+GNN outperforms all methods — achieving the highest throughput (+6.66%), lowest latency (−20.2%), and lowest packet loss (−28.8%) compared to OSPF. Notably, it also beats the theoretical Optimal (1/BW) on throughput and latency, because PPO+GNN learns to balance traffic across multiple paths rather than just minimizing single-path weight.

Why PPO+GNN > Optimal (1/BW)?

The Optimal method uses weight = 1000/bandwidth — a static heuristic. PPO+GNN learns dynamic routing policies that consider traffic patterns, load balancing, and multi-commodity flow optimization.

ECMP Strengths & Weaknesses

ECMP achieves the best latency reduction (−33.4%) through load balancing, but barely improves throughput (+0.78%) because it doesn't account for link capacity differences.

🧠 AI Decision Criteria

PPO+GNN learned a clear policy: inverse relationship between bandwidth and weight. Wide links (100-200 Mbps) receive weights 1.1–5.5, while narrow links (15-20 Mbps) receive weights 50–80. OSPF assigns equal weight (1.0) to all links, ignoring capacity differences entirely.
